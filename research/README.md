## Distributional Regime Persistence

DRP develops geometric measures of persistence, change, and stress in financial return distributions. Its contribution centres on measurement and estimator design.

| Research strand | Focus |
| :--- | :--- |
| Distributional regime persistence | Market memory represented through empirical distributions |
| Real-time regime monitoring | Sequential distributional change and stress detection |
| Scale, shape, location, and speed | Decomposition of distributional movements |
| Variance memory and distributional memory | Distinct persistence objects and their empirical relationship |
| Later DRP work | Portability, calibration, and estimator behaviour across markets |

[DRP programme website](https://drp.garch.institute/) · [Paper catalogue](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/cf_dev/AbsByAuth.cfm?per_id=11814228)

## Volatility and crypto markets

Research connects GARCH-family volatility models with high-frequency measures of apparent roughness and derivatives-market conditions.

The crypto roughness series covers four related questions:

1. Perpetual-futures funding and apparent roughness.
2. Comparisons across roughness estimators.
3. Funding sign and asymmetry.
4. Realized quarticity, normalized tail shape, and forecasting.

Each paper has its own estimand and evidence. The funding-sign and tail-risk studies remain separate entries in future code releases.

## Japan macro-finance

The Japan series connects interest rates, exchange rates, intervention, and cross-border portfolio flows.

| Study theme | Question |
| :--- | :--- |
| JGB yield shocks | Which yield movements represent changes in carry conditions? |
| Carry unwinds | What joint market evidence identifies an unwind? |
| Yen intervention | How do different shocks generate similar exchange-rate moves? |
| Repatriation | How do portfolio flows inform the interpretation of currency movements? |
| Intervention timing | How does intervention selection shape observed outcomes? |

## Monetary policy communication

This research examines the volatility content of FOMC language, voting rights, and policy projections. It connects textual measures and institutional features with financial-market responses.

## Climate risk and AI

Research covers climate-risk transmission through credit ratings and corporate bonds, disclosure and information availability, heat exposure and AI-related activity, and the financial implications of AI adoption.

## Market design and institutions

Topics include Hong Kong IPO cornerstone participation and lockups, allocation reform, market access and trading constraints, and emerging compute derivatives.
