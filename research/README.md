## Distributional Regime Persistence

DRP develops geometric measures of persistence, change, and stress in financial return distributions. Its contribution centres on measurement and estimator design.

| Research strand | Focus |
| :--- | :--- |
| Distributional regime persistence | Market memory represented through empirical distributions |
| Real-time regime monitoring | Sequential distributional change and stress detection |
| Scale, shape, location, and speed | Decomposition of distributional movements |
| Variance memory and distributional memory | Distinct persistence objects and their empirical relationship |
| Adaptive complexity and prototype identity | When a two-prototype representation is too compressed, and how many prototypes the geometry supports |

### Real-time stress monitoring

**DRP2 — [Real-Time Distributional Regime Monitoring: Online Wasserstein Detection of Market Stress](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7245378)** · Fuli Yang and Tingting Qiu · last revised October 18, 2026.

The current public abstract adds a direct comparison with past-only Kolmogorov–Smirnov monitors and assesses prototype-based stress measurement through event coverage, response time and alarm duration. ### Conditional tail forecasting

**DRP5 — [Geometric Stress Coordinates for Conditional Tail Forecasting: Soft Distributional Routing across Equity Markets](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7375598)** · SSRN 7375598 · 2026.

Tests the role of geometric stress coordinates in conditional tail forecasting. Its scope is conditional on the scale filter, market, and estimation memory; comparative forecast-loss gains do not establish universal portability or absolute calibration.

## Volatility and crypto markets

Research connects GARCH-family volatility models with high-frequency measures of apparent roughness and derivatives-market conditions.

The crypto roughness series covers four related questions:

1. Perpetual-futures funding and apparent roughness.
2. Comparisons across roughness estimators.
3. Funding sign and asymmetry.
4. Realized quarticity, normalized tail shape, and forecasting.

Each paper has its own estimand and evidence. The funding-sign and tail-risk studies remain separate entries in future code releases.
